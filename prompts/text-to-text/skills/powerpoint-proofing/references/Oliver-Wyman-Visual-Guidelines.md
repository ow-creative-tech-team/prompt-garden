# Oliver Wyman visual guidelines for PowerPoint

## Brand-system essentials

- Build presentations around clarity, confidence, precision, expertise, perspective, and humanity.
- Use the core palette, approved typography, assets, and logo files consistently. Keep layouts focused, legible, balanced, and generous in white space.
- Do not create new colors, apply gradients, transparency, shadows, or effects, or use unapproved/recreated brand assets.
- Ensure slide text, data visualizations, and tables meet sufficient contrast requirements in line with ADA and WCAG 2.1.

## Typography

### Font roles

| Role | Font | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Marsh Serif Regular or Italic (400) | Headlines, section titles, large quotes/statements, large numerals, and key figures |
| Supporting | Noto Sans | Subheads, captions, body copy, paragraphs, supporting labels, and data points |

- Use Marsh Serif generously and at large scale for short, high-impact messages; it should convey authority without being used for body copy.
- Use Noto Sans to keep supporting and longer text clear and approachable. Available weights are 300, 400, 500, and 700, each in normal and italic styles.
- Set presentation copy in sentence case.
- Set type predominantly in Midnight Blue, Sky Blue, or White. Use traffic colors only selectively for positive or negative data.
- Prioritize maximum legibility: clean alignment, balanced spacing, intuitive hierarchy, and consistent leading, tracking, and alignment.
- Use size, weight, and color purposefully to guide attention. Do not mix too many type sizes or weights in one layout.

### Quotes and alternatives

- Use the approved Marsh-symbol quotation-mark artwork only for clear, large-scale visual impact; never recreate or alter it.
- The graphic quotation marks must touch the top and bottom edges of the screen, canvas, or frame; never float them in the layout.
- If the graphic marks cannot be used, use Marsh Serif quotation marks for display copy and Noto Sans quotation marks for body copy.
- Where Marsh Serif does not support the script, use the appropriate Noto Serif language cut: Noto Serif JP (Japanese), KR (Korean), or SC (Simplified Chinese).
- For rare system-font-only situations, use Georgia Pro Light for headlines (Georgia if only legacy fonts are available) and Arial for body copy when Noto Sans is unavailable.

### Avoid

- Previous typefaces, including MMC Display.
- Substitute serif or sans-serif fonts except the approved fallbacks.
- Distorting or over-stylizing type.
- Overly tight or loose leading.

## Color

### Use principles

- Lead with Sky Blue, Midnight Blue, and White. Sky Blue brings openness and approachability; Midnight Blue brings depth and confidence.
- Use Gold 750 sparingly as the active/action color for navigation, important information, focus, or refinement. It should highlight, never dominate.
- Use secondary colors only where they add meaning, especially in data visualization, patterns, and illustration. Blue is the main secondary color for data visualization; neutrals can structure layouts; Green and Purple are least prominent and reserved for complex data sets.
- Use traffic colors according to their universal meanings: green = positive/success, yellow = caution/attention, red = negative/critical.
- Neutrals may be used as section colors or subtle dividers. Keep white space generous; avoid using too many colors at once.

### Traffic colors

| Meaning | Color | HEX | RGB |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success / positive | Green | `#14853D` | 20, 133, 61 |
| Attention / caution | Yellow | `#FFBE00` | 255, 190, 0 |
| Negative / critical | Crimson | `#C53532` | 197, 53, 50 |

Traffic colors follow the universal logic of traffic lights. Use them only when
they help readers interpret performance, progress, change, or status at a
glance; do not use them as general decoration.

#### Traffic-success vs. support-green distinction

- **Traffic success** is `#14853D`. Use it only when the color communicates a
  positive, successful, improving, or completed state.
- **Support Green 1000** is `#2F7500`. Use it sparingly as a support or series
  color in a complex dataset without positive/status meaning.
- These colors are distinct tokens and are not interchangeable. Determine the
  intended semantic role from the legend, labels, surrounding copy, and the
  color's relationship to other data.
- Flag traffic-color use only when it is decorative, contradicts the data
  meaning, or is applied inconsistently in a potentially misleading way. If the
  intended meaning cannot be verified, classify the issue as `Unresolved`.

## Logo

### Endorsement and approved variations

- Oliver Wyman is a Marsh sub-brand. Use the Oliver Wyman logo with the endorsement **"A Marsh business"**; do not use the Marsh logo directly.
- Use the supplied master Oliver Wyman lockup artwork; never rebuild, recreate,
  or substitute the Marsh parent logo or Perspective M for it.
- **Midnight Blue** is the primary/default logo for light solid or light photographic backgrounds with sufficient contrast.
- **White** is the default logo on dark backgrounds, photography, textures, and layered imagery, especially when the endorsement needs greater readability or adjacent elements do not use Sky Blue.
- **Sky Blue** is reserved for a controlled, solid Midnight Blue background where surrounding typography and graphic elements are also Sky Blue and the composition is intentionally monochromatic. This is the default treatment in the PowerPoint template.
- Do not use the Sky Blue logo on photography or textured backgrounds, or where surrounding content is white or mixed color.
- **Black** is only for a PowerPoint output that is technically required to be black and white, not as a stylistic preference.

### Protection, placement, and PowerPoint sizing

- Keep clear space equal to the logo cap height on every side.
- Use the standard logo and endorsement lockup in PowerPoint. Use special artwork only for an approved presentation use case.
- Prefer the top-left corner, aligned to the layout margin or grid. Other corners are acceptable when required by the composition. Center the logo only where it is the hero element.
- For endorsed and transition-state logos, the height measurement includes the endorsement line.
- On a PowerPoint cover, set the total endorsed-lockup height to **0.52 in**.
- For non-cover slides, follow the approved PowerPoint template. The supplied
  materials do not define a separate numeric logo height for those layouts.
- No universal maximum logo size is defined. For oversized or full-bleed
  auditorium presentations, use approved extra-large artwork and obtain
  brand-team approval.
- Use approved special artwork for a full-bleed auditorium presentation only
  with brand-team approval. Any endorsement-size adjustment also requires
  brand-team approval.

### Sub-businesses and third parties

- Oliver Wyman is the sole master-brand logo. Oliver Wyman Forum is the only permitted distinct logo alongside it.
- Sub-businesses (for example, Innopay, Veritas, Vector) must be Noto Sans descriptors, clearly separated from the logo through spacing, alignment, and hierarchy. Never extend, combine, or alter the protected logo with text or graphics.
- Use regional logo artwork only in its approved market; for example, the French-Canadian version is for Canadian offices only.
- For an Oliver Wyman-led communication, use an Oliver Wyman-led presentation. For shared ownership, use both logos at equal visual weight, separated by a 1 pt Midnight Blue rule, horizontally or vertically aligned. Center-align both logos and make the divider height match the endorsed-logo height. Apply the same rule when multiple third-party logos are present.

### Never

- Use anything except approved logo files.
- Stretch, distort, rotate, rearrange, recolor, or alter a lockup.
- Add gradients, shadows, or effects.
- Place the logo on a busy or low-contrast background.

## Photography

### Purpose and selection

- Treat photography as evidence of perspective, expertise, and humanity - never as decoration.
- Images should build confidence; demonstrate understanding and a clear point of view; make expertise visible and grounded; show real collaboration; and acknowledge complexity, transition, and decision-making.
- Choose from three categories: **industries and sectors**, **people and lifestyle**, and **conceptual and evocative**. Prioritize the category that best serves the communication objective and audience.
- Industry/sector imagery should be dramatic and show real domain knowledge. People/lifestyle imagery should show communities with honesty, warmth, connection, and real engagement. Conceptual/evocative imagery can use abstract or symbolic subjects to deepen the message when paired with copy.
- Do not use country flags (risk of inaccuracies and misrepresentation), and follow third-party image copyright requirements.

### Perspective and visual style

- Use a range of viewpoints: **macro** (wide/high-angle context), **meso** (human-eye-level, in-the-moment), and **micro** (detail and precision).
- Seek photojournalistic, cinematic-impactful, authentic, focused, metaphorical, and simple/graphic images with copy space.
- Show unique vantage points and natural, unposed moments. Keep compositions uncluttered, with one clear focus and enough open space for type or design elements.
- Avoid artificial or over-retouched imagery, staged/perfect scenes, generic/cliched stock, heavy filters, and dramatic color grading.

### Retouching and portraits

- Retouch subtly: refine rather than transform; retain natural detail and realism.
- For people, add only gentle, natural warmth. Do not warm landscapes to the point of appearing unrealistic.
- Increase saturation/vibrance and contrast only slightly. Use vibrance for people to protect skin tones; preserve highlight and shadow detail.
- Colleague portraits: authentic/personable expression; centered framing; indoor/outdoor natural or diffused light; shallow depth of field; high contrast and medium-high saturation; a warm overall tone; simple, solid-colored clothing. Avoid black, white, stripes, and busy patterns.

## Illustration

### Select the role by intent

| Role | Use when | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Explainer | Explaining what, how, or who; processes, services, instructions, or change | Literal/semi-literal, easy to decode, calm, practical, and instructional |
| Symbolic | Introducing an idea through shared visual language | Signals and orients |
| Metaphorical | Expressing a specific insight or message | Expresses insight |
| Hero | A defining or high-stakes moment | A designation for metaphorical work, not a separate style |

- Explainer illustrations must make information usable, not impress, provoke, or surprise. Use the shared library rather than commissioning bespoke assets for individual requests.
- The illustration style is simple, confident, refined, and rooted in the logo visual language. Combine color fills with outlined shapes/forms; use clean space, simple balanced compositions, and clear hierarchy.
- Use core and neutral colors plus **one** secondary color. Use that secondary color selectively to emphasize the important element or clarify the story. Never use multiple secondary colors in one illustration or make a secondary color the background.
- Explainer and Symbolic assets in the Library must be used as provided: do not modify, deconstruct, or independently recreate them. If a suitable asset is absent, consult the Creative Studio.

## Icons

- Use functional icons only to support slide navigation, actions, or simple labels - never to communicate complex concepts, act as standalone brand marks, or serve as decoration.
- Source icons from the Google Material Symbols library. Use the **Material Symbols (new)**, **Outlined** style.
- Use Midnight Blue on light backgrounds. Use White or Sky Blue on dark backgrounds.
- Apply these settings: fill off, weight **300**, grade **0**, and optical size **24-48**.
- Do not use filled icon styles, secondary colors, decorative/illustrative details, gradients, shadows, effects, or icons as conceptual symbols.
